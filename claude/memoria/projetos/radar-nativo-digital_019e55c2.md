# Memória do projeto: Radar Nativo Digital

**Purpose & context**

Daniel is conducting structured market research on Brazilian fashion and apparel brands across multiple niches — including men's fashion, fitness/bodybuilding, and combat sports fightwear. The work appears oriented toward building vetted brand pipelines, likely for commercial, investment, or partnership purposes. Success looks like clean, sourced spreadsheets with high data integrity rather than inflated lists.

Key domain areas: Brazilian DTC/apparel market, Instagram-based brand discovery, cluster/positioning analysis (AB medium-high), regional retail context (e.g., Iguatemi POA/Brasília).

**Current state**

Three research tracks have been completed, each resulting in a formatted Excel deliverable:

- **Men's fashion**: 12 verified brands meeting relaxed criteria (post-2016, 15K+ followers, Regional premium cluster). Top fits identified as Sardegna Sartoria, CARNAN, and P. Andrade.
- **Bodybuilding (musculação)**: ~19–20 brands across confidence tiers, with only one fully meeting all four original criteria. Overall confidence rated 7/10.
- **Combat sports fightwear**: 13 validated Brazilian-owned brands (down from a 20-brand target), ranked by followers from KVRA (235K) to Naja Extreme (17K). Gap explained by foreign ownership of well-known brands.

In all three cases, original targets of 20 brands could not be met without compromising data integrity, and Daniel consistently accepted honest shortfalls over inflated results.

**On the horizon**

- Potential expansion of fightwear list by either lowering follower threshold to 5K or broadening scope to athleisure brands with combat-sport aesthetics.
- Potential relaxation of founding year cutoff for bodybuilding segment (≥2015 recommended) to yield a more actionable pipeline.
- Additional niche segments may be researched following the same methodology.

**Key learnings & principles**

- The intersection of strict criteria (Brazilian origin + niche focus + follower minimums + founding year cutoffs) consistently yields fewer brands than targeted — this is a structural reality of the Brazilian market, not a research failure.
- Foreign ownership is a meaningful filter: several prominent "Brazilian-feeling" brands (Venum, Hayabusa, Albino & Preto, Shoyoroll, Bull Terrier) are not Brazilian-owned.
- CNPJ verification via Receita Federal (Serasa/Econodata) is the gold standard for confirming Brazilian origin; faturamento, investment, and collaboration data are generally not publicly available without paid databases (e.g., Pitchbook, CB Insights).
- Daniel prefers iterative criteria relaxation with transparency over padding results to hit a number.

**Approach & patterns**

- Daniel provides research briefs using templates, sometimes with placeholder values that require clarification before execution — Claude should confirm key parameters (follower threshold, brand count, founding year, cluster) before proceeding.
- Deliverables are structured Excel files with: a main data tab ordered by Instagram followers descending, per-row confidence scores (1–10), source/observation columns, and a methodology/criteria tab.
- Color coding is used to distinguish confidence tiers (green = full match, yellow = partial, red = reference-only).
- Transparency about data gaps and confidence levels is expected and valued throughout.

**Tools & resources**

- Instagram as primary discovery and validation signal.
- Receita Federal / Serasa / Econodata for CNPJ-based origin verification.
- Excel (.xlsx) as the standard deliverable format.
