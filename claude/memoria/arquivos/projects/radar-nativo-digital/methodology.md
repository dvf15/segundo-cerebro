## Working approach

- [stated] Daniel provides research briefs using templates, sometimes with placeholder values that require clarification before execution — Claude should confirm key parameters (follower threshold, brand count, founding year, cluster) before proceeding
- [stated] Deliverables are structured Excel files with a main data tab ordered by Instagram followers descending, per-row confidence scores (1–10), source/observation columns, and a methodology/criteria tab
- [stated] Color coding distinguishes confidence tiers: green = full match, yellow = partial, red = reference-only
- [stated] Transparency about data gaps and confidence levels is expected and valued throughout
- [stated] Daniel prefers iterative criteria relaxation with transparency over padding results to hit a number

## Key learnings

- [stated] The intersection of strict criteria (Brazilian origin + niche focus + follower minimums + founding year cutoffs) consistently yields fewer brands than targeted — a structural reality of the Brazilian market, not a research failure
- [stated] Foreign ownership is a meaningful filter: several prominent "Brazilian-feeling" brands (Venum, Hayabusa, Albino & Preto, Shoyoroll, Bull Terrier) are not Brazilian-owned
- [stated] CNPJ verification via Receita Federal (Serasa/Econodata) is the gold standard for confirming Brazilian origin
- [stated] Faturamento, investment, and collaboration data are generally not publicly available without paid databases such as Pitchbook or CB Insights

## Tools & resources

- [stated] Instagram is the primary discovery and validation signal
- [stated] Receita Federal / Serasa / Econodata are used for CNPJ-based origin verification
- [stated] Excel (.xlsx) is the standard deliverable format
